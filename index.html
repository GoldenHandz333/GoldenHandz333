Field Guide: A Beginner's Intro to Creating a Mini Food Forest

Built from what's actually in dah arsenal — no store-bought systems required. Every step below was done on this land and photographed above.

  1. Walk dah land first. Read what's already established — ʻulu, banana, mature trees. Honor what dah land gives you before you change anything.
  2. Clear in sections. Take dah overgrowth down piece by piece, not all at once. What you cut becomes mulch and edging later.
  3. Strip dah synthetics. Pull old weed barrier and tarps. They choke dah soil biology you're trying to build.
  4. Start dah nursery. Pot up starts and stage them — they wait while you build dah beds. A nursery row keeps everything organized.
  5. Open dah beds. Expose that dark volcanic soil. Edge with what you have — rock, logs, whatever dah land provides.
  6. Lay paths from scrap. Roofing sheets, boards, mulch — paths keep you off dah beds and dah work moving.
  7. Mulch heavy. Thick mulch locks moisture in. Dah foundation rode through two storms — Lala, then Nola — with no damage.
  8. Plant dah staples. Kalo in dah ground first, then companions: beans, chard, greens, turmeric, green onions. Polyculture, not monoculture.
  9. Catch your water. A simple catchment tub near dah beds means you're not hauling water across dah property.
  10. Document every step. Photo, note, date. That's dah journal — and that's dah teaching.
